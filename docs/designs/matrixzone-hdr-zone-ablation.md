# Design: matrixZone HDR, measure what the zones buy

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-03
Branch: feat/pixal3d-object-hdr
Repo: mikejamesvfx/atlas-camera
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

The branch ships three tracks (Pixal3D hidden sides, AtlasSceneTo3D, matrixZone
SDR->HDR). The user picked the **8K HDR plate** as the headline. As built, a 2x2
matrixZone run on the 8K machine plate takes 14 min 27 s cold on an RTX 5090: one
global pass plus four zones, five LTX clips. Its output is Nuke-confirmed, with a seam
step test worst of 0.88x.

The default clean-up is `sdr_detail_transfer` in `core/matrixzone.py`. Outside the
SDR-clipped area it rebuilds pixels as `sdr * 2^ratio_smooth`. The ratio
`log2(hdr) - log2(sdr)` goes through a guided filter at radius 64 px, plate resolution,
guided by SDR log luminance. Its effective support is about 2r = 128 px, because the
slope and offset are box-averaged again. Clipped highlights keep the HDR pixels.
Nobody has measured how much of the delivered EXR outside the clipped area depends on
the 4K-tier zones and not on the render/4 global pass. If little does, most of the
runtime goes to pixels the last step discards.

## What Makes This Cool

An SDR still becomes a plate-resolution ACEScg EXR a compositor can grade. Running the
LTX-2.5 HDR IC-LoRA directly, the research graph's ~0.9 MP cap gave about 1280 px. That
was the local baseline this work replaced. No claim is made about other tools.

## Constraints

- Combo values and widget order are append-only (saved-workflow contract).
- The EXR stays labelled a MODEL RECONSTRUCTION, never photographed HDR.
- Registry publish stays gated; no version bump without an explicit go-ahead.
- `core/` stays host-agnostic (numpy only) and gains only maths that ships.
- Any shipped planner keeps parity with `atlas_bridge.matrixzone.plan()`.

## Premises (agreed; #2 revised after the cross-model challenge)

1. The headline user is a compositor who needs a plate-resolution HDR EXR from an SDR
   still, labelled as a model inference.
2. (revised) Outside SDR-clipped regions, the zones contribute only through the
   guided-filter slope and offset of the smoothed log ratio (about 128 px support).
   Whether that differs visibly from what the global pass alone gives is UNKNOWN, and it
   is the first thing to measure.
3. If the difference is negligible, "global pass + crops only where the SDR clips" could
   deliver the same EXR faster. Measured, not assumed.
4. The branch merges as one unit, and registry publish stays gated.

## Cross-Model Perspective (Codex)

- Coolest version: a layered EXR (SDR-derived base, inferred gain field, highlight
  reconstruction, inference mask), so comp can grade the invented highlights or reject
  them per region.
- Challenged premise #2: the zone HDR feeds the guided filter's slope and offset
  everywhere, so smoothing reduces the dependency but does not remove it. Also,
  `kept_hdr_fraction` counts `keep > 0.5`, not every pixel the HDR influences.
- A 14% clipped mask can touch all four 2x2 quadrants, so skipping whole quadrants may
  save nothing. The speedup needs crop scheduling around the mask.
- Prior art: RUDRA (analytic inverse curve plus a learned residual).
- Prototype: a comparison harness on cached results first, scheduling second.

## Approaches Considered

- **A, ablation harness first (CHOSEN):** offline, on cached intermediates. Effort S,
  risk low.
- **B, sparse zones:** LTX runs only on crops covering the clipped mask; the global pass
  anchors the rest. Effort L, risk medium. Gated on A.
- **C, plate curve + LTX only for highlights:** a monotone SDR->HDR curve fitted from the
  global pass (the AtlasHDRVertexTransfer fit) outside the mask. Effort M, risk medium.
  Gated on A.
- **Layered EXR:** DEFERRED. It is independent of A, B and C, so it gets its own design
  once the speed question is settled.

## Recommended Approach: A

### Capturing the intermediates
Today `AtlasMatrixZoneStitch` persists nothing besides the final EXR, and ComfyUI's
cache lives only in memory. Add one debug dump, gated by the environment variable
`ATLAS_MATRIXZONE_DUMP_DIR` (the same pattern as `ATLAS_AGENT_MODE`; no widget, no combo):
- When it is set, the stitch writes `global.npy` and `zone_XX.npy` (float32 `hdr_linear`
  as received), `sdr.npy` (the display plate), and `plan.json` (the ATLAS_MATRIXZONE
  handle).
- When it is unset, nothing changes. A test pins that the unset path writes no files.

### Masks (all at plate resolution; `keep` = `sdr_detail_transfer`'s own feathered mask)
- `keep` is the box-mean (r 4) of the SDR max channel, mapped through the clip window
  (0.85 to 0.97) with smoothstep. It is identical to the shipped function's, factored
  out so the harness and the function share one definition.
- **Clipped core** C = `keep > 0.5`.
- **Transition** T = dilate(`keep > 0`, 128 px) minus C. 128 px is the guided filter's 2r
  support at radius 64.
- **Unclipped** U = everything not in C or T. C, T and U are disjoint and cover the plate.

### Variants (every one goes through the SAME chain: stitch -> band destripe -> local destripe -> `sdr_detail_transfer`)
- **V1 full:** the cached zones and global pass, exactly as live. This is the reference.
- **V2 global-ratio:** each zone input replaced by the crop of the upsampled global HDR at
  that zone's renderRect. The seam test on V2 is seam-free by construction, so only
  region stops count.
- **V3 global + clipped crops:** zone pixels inside dilate(C, 128 px + 128 px overlap
  feather), the upsampled global elsewhere, blended in log space with the same 128 px
  feather. V3 is an UPPER BOUND on B's quality: its crop pixels are the full run's,
  and fresh smaller crops would see less context.
- **V4 global curve:** a monotone SDR->HDR curve fitted on the global pass (the existing
  AtlasHDRVertexTransfer fit) and applied to the SDR in U and T; zone pixels in C. This
  is the variant that measures C.

### Metrics
- Per variant vs V1: |log2 luminance| stops, p50/p95/p99, in C, T and U separately.
- Seam step test worst score per variant (V1 = 0.88x).
- Exposure sweep PNGs at -3, 0 and +3 stops per variant.
- Crop accounting for V3: a greedy cover of dilate(C) with zone-tier crops (3904x2240,
  64-clean origins, 128 px overlap), merging blobs that share a crop. Report the crop
  count and area. This cover is accounting only. A shipped B would add the mode to
  `plan()` itself and pin parity there.
- Runtime model: the live run is timed per clip from ComfyUI's per-node timings (load,
  global, each zone). Projected B time = load + global + crop count x per-crop time. It is
  not scaled by area.

### Decision rule (cheapest first; must pass on BOTH plates)
Pass bar for a variant: U p99 <= 0.05 stops and T p99 <= 0.10 stops, seam worst <= 1.0x (on the WINDOWED seam score from T5; recalibrate after the first live re-score),
and no difference the user can see in Nuke at -3/0/+3 on the sweep. The thresholds are
PROVISIONAL. 0.05 stops is about 3.5% in linear, below a typical grade nudge, to be
revisited after the first run.
1. If V4 passes, build C (fastest).
2. Else if V2 passes outside C, build a "global-ratio + clipped crops" mode (B without
   zone crops outside C).
3. Else if V3 passes AND projected B time is <= 1/3 of the live time, build B, but only
   after one live LTX run on a single scheduled crop matches V3 in C within T's bar
   (this closes V3's upper-bound gap).
4. Else the zones change the output materially. Before concluding they are worth their
   runtime, the user does a blind A/B in Nuke of V1 vs V2. If V2 is judged as good,
   go to 2. Otherwise keep full and close the speedup track.

### Code placement
- `core/matrixzone.py` gains only the factored-out `keep` mask (shared with
  `sdr_detail_transfer`) and the dump hook it calls.
- Region masks, variants, the cover and the metrics live in a new ablation script under `tools/` (not yet written).

### Tests (synthetic, numpy only)
- V1 rebuilt from the dump equals the live stitch output (max rel. error < 1e-5).
- Identical zone = global input gives V2 == V1 exactly.
- A perturbation injected only in one zone's U region is reported in U, not C or T.
- C, T and U are disjoint and cover the plate.
- Refactoring `keep` leaves `sdr_detail_transfer`'s output byte-identical.
- With `ATLAS_MATRIXZONE_DUMP_DIR` unset, the stitch writes no files.

  the decision rule yields exactly one outcome.

## Reviewer Concerns

Disposition: CONCERNS_RECORDED

Stop: CONVERGENCE

### R2-1 — clarity

**Problem**

> The heading says every variant goes through the same chain (stitch -> band destripe -> local destripe -> sdr_detail_transfer), but only V2 is specified as zone inputs. V3 blends zone and global 'in log space' without saying whether this happens on the zone inputs before the stitch or on the output. V4 is described by output region ('applied to the SDR in U and T; zone pixels in C'), which does not fit a chain that starts at the stitch: it is unclear whether the curve output is fed through sdr_detail_transfer again or bypasses it. The document also does not say what the seam test can detect for V3 and V4.

**Remedy**

> For V3 and V4, state what is fed into each zone slot of the stitch, at which stage the blend or curve is applied, and whether sdr_detail_transfer runs on the result. For each variant, state what the seam score can and cannot detect.

### R2-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> Decision step 2 builds 'global-ratio + clipped crops (B without zone crops outside C)'. Approach B is already defined as 'LTX runs only on crops covering the clipped mask; the global pass anchors the rest', so steps 2 and 3 build the same thing. Step 2 nonetheless skips the runtime gate (<= 1/3) and the live-crop check that step 3 requires.

**Remedy**

> Either merge steps 2 and 3 into one 'build B' outcome with a single set of gates, or state exactly how the step 2 mode differs from B (for example, it uses the global ratio rather than the global pixels outside C) and why it is exempt from the runtime and live-crop gates.

### R2-3 — feasibility

**Problem**

> Steps 1 (V4 -> C) and 2 build modes that still run LTX on crops in C. The evidence for C quality comes only from the cached full-run zone pixels, which is the same upper-bound gap the document acknowledges for V3. Only step 3 requires a live crop check and a projected-runtime gate, so C or the step-2 mode could be built without a check that fresh crops reproduce the result in C and without a check that they are actually faster.

**Remedy**

> Apply the live single-crop check, and a projected-runtime bar, to every outcome that schedules LTX crops in C (steps 1, 2 and 3), or justify exempting them.

### R2-4 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The crop accounting uses zone-tier crops (3904x2240), the same size as a zone, so per-crop time is roughly per-zone time. With the projection load + global + n x zone, the step 3 bar (<= 1/3 of load + global + 4 x zone) needs 2(load + global) + 3n x zone <= 4 x zone. That bar is unreachable for n >= 1 unless load and global are tiny. Step 3, and therefore B, may never be reachable, which the document does not acknowledge.

**Remedy**

> Justify the 1/3 bar against the expected load/global/zone split, allow smaller crop tiers in the cover, or state plainly that B is only reachable when the cover needs 0 or 1 crops.

### R2-5 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The runtime model relies on 'ComfyUI's per-node timings (load, global, each zone)'. Stock ComfyUI reports total prompt execution time, not a per-node breakdown, and the zone clips may run inside one node or loop, so per-zone times may not be separable. The document names no mechanism that produces these numbers.

**Remedy**

> Name the timing source (for example, explicit timers in the dump hook, log timestamps per LTX clip, or a profiling extension), and confirm it separates model load, the global pass and each zone.

### R2-6 — consistency

**Problem**

> Code placement puts 'the dump hook' in core/matrixzone.py, but the hook reads an environment variable and writes .npy/.json files. That is I/O and host behaviour, not 'maths that ships', so it conflicts with the constraint 'core/ ... gains only maths that ships'. The capture section also says the stitch (AtlasMatrixZoneStitch, a comfy node) does the writing, so the hook's home is contradictory.

**Remedy**

> Put the env-var check and file writing in the comfy stitch node (or a non-core helper), and keep core limited to the factored keep mask. Alternatively, amend the constraint and justify the exception.

### R2-7 — completeness

**Problem**

> The dump writes global/zone hdr_linear, sdr.npy and plan.json, but not the stitch and clean-up parameters V1's replay depends on: destripe settings, the clip window, the guided-filter radius and any widget values. The test 'V1 rebuilt from the dump equals the live stitch output' and the offline harness need those values. plan.json is the planner handle, and the document does not show that it carries them. It also does not confirm that the ATLAS_MATRIXZONE handle is JSON-serialisable.

**Remedy**

> Add a params.json (or extend plan.json) that records every stitch, destripe and transfer parameter used live, and confirm that the plan handle serialises to JSON losslessly.

### R2-8 — feasibility

**Problem**

> V4 reuses 'the existing AtlasHDRVertexTransfer fit'. That is a comfy node fit (on vertices), but the harness lives in tools/. Under the layering rule nothing outside comfy/ may import comfy, and the document does not say whether the fit is reachable from core or how a vertex-based fit applies to plate pixels.

**Remedy**

> State where the monotone-curve fit lives and that tools/ can call it without importing comfy/ (moving it to core if B or C would ship it). Confirm it accepts per-pixel SDR/HDR samples.

### R2-9 — clarity

**Problem**

> The decision rule leaves gaps. (a) Step 3 gives no outcome when the live single-crop check fails. (b) Step 4's 'If V2 is judged as good, go to 2' sends the flow back to a step whose quantitative bar V2 has already failed, without saying whether the A/B overrides the numeric bar. (c) The blind A/B gives no protocol: who randomises and hides the labels when the same user judges.

**Remedy**

> Give an explicit outcome for a failed live-crop check. State that a passing blind A/B overrides step 2's numeric bar (or route to a different outcome). Specify the A/B protocol, for example harness-randomised, unlabelled EXRs at -3/0/+3.

### R2-10 — clarity

**Problem**

> The success criterion says the harness 'prints ... and the decision rule yields exactly one outcome', but the pass bar includes a human Nuke judgement and step 4 needs a blind A/B. The harness alone cannot produce the outcome, and the criterion does not say where the human verdicts are recorded.

**Remedy**

> Reword the criterion: the harness prints the numeric gates, and the outcome is recorded after the human sweep or A/B verdict, with that verdict logged next to the numbers in the proposal doc.
